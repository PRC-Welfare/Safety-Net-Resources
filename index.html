★ Our First Step in rehoming

Home-To-Home Direct Rehoming

88%
Success Rate Direct Home-to-Home

Home-to-Home is managed directly by the Spartanburg County Pet Resource Center (PRC) team. Our staff is dedicated to helping you connect with the right adopter so your pet can transition smoothly into their new home—without ever having to enter the shelter!

🛡️ How the Program Works (Simple & Free):

  • 1 Create Your Pet's Profile: Upload clear photos and write a brief biography about your pet's personality and routine.
  • 2 PRC Review & Listing: Our team approves the profile on our side and publishes it under the Spartanburg County PRC portal so prospective adopters across the region can view them.
  • 3 You Stay in Full Control: When an adopter expresses interest, you receive instant text and email notifications with their contact details. You speak directly with potential adopters, arrange meet-and-greets, and choose the best home.
  • 4 Dedicated Safety Net Support: While your pet is looking for a new home, one of our Safety Net Specialists will check in about once a month, provide guidance, and offer free supplies or food to support you.
Sign Up on Home-to-Home (PRC Portal) → Direct Link: https://home-home.org/shelters/sc-prc
📸 Tips for Creating a Great Pet Profile
✨ Highlight the Positives First:

Mention things like crate trained, house trained (or litter trained), basic commands (sit, stay, down), walks well on leash, good with children or other pets, age, and weight.

🤝 Be Honest About Challenges:

Share important details such as not being good with other pets, having anxiety, or being able to jump low fences. Honesty ensures a safe, lasting placement.

📷 Use Quality, Friendly Photos:

Natural daylight (avoid harsh glare) works best. Snap photos at your pet's eye level, and capture everyday moments—playing, lounging, or looking straight at the camera.

🐾 Why Trap-Neuter-Return (TNR) Saves Lives

Almost 100% Euthanasia Risk in Shelters

Did you know that almost 100% of unsocialized community cats surrendered to traditional shelters are euthanized due to their unadoptable behavior? Keeping them in their outdoor territory through TNR saves their lives.

Rapid Feline Reproduction

An average female community cat can have at least 3 litters a year and can become pregnant as early as 4 months of age. Proactive spay/neuter is essential.

Proven Power of TNR

Trap-Neuter-Return (TNR) stabilizes colony populations, stops spraying, yowling, and fighting, improves feline health, and prevents litters of kittens from entering shelters.

Natural Rodent Control & Resilient Allies

Community cats are resilient, adaptive animals! Proper colony care helps reduce the burden on local rescues and shelters while providing natural rodent and pest control.

The Universal Left Ear-Tip

A clean 3/8-inch flat cut off the tip of the left ear while the cat is safely under anesthesia. It is the universal, painless sign that the cat is sterilized and vaccinated.

🥣 Community Cat Feeding & Winter Care
Choose One Dedicated Place

Feed community cats in one quiet, consistent place every day and keep the area spotless to maintain neighborhood harmony.

Provide Fresh Water Daily

Provide fresh water every day. Use wider, deeper dark plastic bowls in freezing winter months to delay ice formation.

Remove Uneaten Food (30-Minute Rule)

Pick up any uneaten food after 30 minutes. Leaving food out overnight attracts insects, raccoons, possums, and other wildlife.

Provide Safe, Warm & Dry Shelters

Provide waterproof shelters packed with clean straw (never hay or blankets, which absorb moisture and freeze). You can buy or easily build simple plastic tote shelters.

🌿 Neighborhood Coexistence & Humane Deterrents
Natural Scent Deterrents

Use humane deterrents to keep cats away from unwanted areas. Fresh orange or lemon peels, wet ground coffee, and natural plant oils like lavender, lemongrass, citronella, or eucalyptus work wonders.

Secure Outdoor Trash Bins

Fasten trash can lids tightly with secure latches or heavy-duty bungee cords so curious animals cannot forage.

Work Collaboratively with Neighbors

Talk proactively with neighbors to address concerns about flower beds, porches, or vehicles. Community cat issues are easily solved with open communication and deterrents.

Stay in Communication

Timely updates keep everyone supported

  • Respond to Pet Resource Center staff within 48 hours of contact.
  • Promptly update us if your housing, financial, or pet situation changes.
  • Keep us informed on your pet’s behavioral or medical progress.

Follow Through on the Plan

Collaboration ensures long-term safety

  • Complete agreed action steps (veterinary appointments, behavioral training, rehoming posts).
  • Actively utilize the food, supplies, and referral resources provided.
  • Reach out and ask questions anytime you need assistance or encounter obstacles.

Care for Your Pet

Daily essentials and health care

  • Provide continuous clean food, fresh water, and comfortable weather-protected shelter.
  • Seek timely veterinary care whenever health issues, illness, or injuries arise.
  • Maintain an enriched, safe, escape-proof living environment.

Use Resources Responsibly

Fair community access

  • Safety-net support services and food pantry distributions are designed as limited, temporary lifelines.
  • Programs like our pet food pantry maintain specific monthly usage limits so all local families can be served.

Be Respectful

A culture of mutual empathy

  • Treat PRC staff, animal control personnel, and shelter rescue partners with courtesy and patience.
  • Our team will always treat you and your family with dignity, confidentiality, and kindness.

Important to Know

Managed intake philosophy

  • We operate as a managed intake shelter (scheduled intake appointments are mandatory).
  • Our primary mission is proactive: keeping loving pets in their existing homes first whenever possible.
  • When keeping the pet is truly not an option, we partner closely to guide and assist with safe rehoming.
